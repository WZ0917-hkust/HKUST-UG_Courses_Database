# Description
Reading Course: Derived Categories and Geometric Invariant Theory 
This reading course is ideal for students enroled in Algebraic Geometry II or those pursuing algebraic geometry studies independently. It primarily addresses two topics: I. Derived Categories and II. Geometric invariant theory (GIT). Additionally, the course explores the connections between these topics, focusing on III. Derived categories of GIT quotients, as developed by Halpern-Leistner [HL] and Ballard, Favero and Katzarkov [BFK]. Derived categories, first introduced by Grothendieck and Verdier for studying Grothendieckâs duality, have since become indispensable tools in algebraic geometry, representing fundamental invariants of algebraic varieties. Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT) plays a critical role in studying quotients in algebraic geometry and constructing moduli spaces. A seminal result in the study of GIT quotients is the Kirwan surjectivity theorem, which establishes a surjective map from the equivariant cohomology of a space X to its GIT quotient X//G, with an explicitly y understood kernel. This reading course aims to explore a categorification of this surjectivity, as developed by [HL] and [BFK], which has broad implications in algebraic geometry, representation theory, and mathematical physics. This course will operate in a seminar/presentation format, where each participant is expected to lead one or two sessions. Each session will span approximately 2 to 3 hours. Students should seek approval from the course instructor to take this reading course.
