# Description
Applications of Psychology and Economics 
This course introduces the psychological evidence as the basis for sound economic analysis, building extensions to the neoclassical theories as a benchmark. Our approach will combine elements of applied microeconomic theory and evidence from field, lab and natural experiments, with an emphasis on the empirical studies. We will discuss examples drawn from a variety of fields: asset pricing, corporate finance, consumption, personnel economics, development economics, environmental economics, health economics, political economy, and public economics. Integrated with the research topics, students will also be trained in empirical techniques in data analysis.
